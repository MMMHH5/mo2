Tel-Agent — colour palette

Tel-Agent's own identity, independent of the Dpro brand. Dark theme first. Every contrast ratio below is measured, not estimated.

Why violet — by elimination, not taste

The design specification already assigns three of the four colours it needs: green for passed to a human, red for blocked, amber for needs attention. The accent is the fourth, and it means the AI handled this.

So the accent has to sit far from all three in hue, or the handling badges start lying to the reader. That removes green, red, orange and yellow immediately. What is left is blue, teal, violet and magenta.

  • Teal — too close to green at badge size. A teal “AI handled” chip and a green “passed to human” chip are confusable in peripheral vision, which is exactly how badges get read.
  • Blue — safe, and the default accent of every developer tool. It also reads as “information”, which weakens it as a state colour.
  • Magenta — distinctive, but reads consumer and playful. Wrong register for something a clinic runs.
  • Violet — maximally distant from all three semantics, distinctive without being loud, and it holds up on both dark and light grounds.

Everything meant to carry text clears WCAG AA (4.5:1) with room to spare. dim at 3.9 is the one exception and is deliberate — it is for timestamps and column labels only, and must never be used for anything a person actually reads.

The semantic set in context

AI handled Passed to human Blocked Needs attention LIVE CALL

Four states, four hues, no two confusable at a glance or in peripheral vision. The live indicator pulses, as the specification requires — it is the only place the accent is allowed to move.

The palette applied to the transcript

Worth noticing: the transcript needs three speaker colours, and it does not need three new ones. The semantic set already answers the question.

Whisper — not heard by the caller
00:44   Operator → Agent  ·  The referral is valid until 30 September. Tell her she does not need a new one.

Caller takes muted grey — they speak the most lines, and colouring them would tire the eye over a long transcript. Agent takes the accent, matching the “AI handled” badge in the header. Human operator takes green, matching “passed to a human”. The takeover rules are green for the same reason. One vocabulary, learned once.

The whisper channel deliberately gets no semantic colour at all — a dashed border and muted text instead. Amber was tempting and would have been wrong: whisper is not a problem, and amber has to keep meaning “something needs you”.

Light theme

A real second pass, not an inversion. The accent and semantics darken so they hold contrast on white; the hue relationships stay identical.

Note that amber on light must become a dark ochre. The bright #FBBF24 that works on dark scores about 1.7 on white — invisible. This is the clearest proof that the light theme cannot be generated by inverting the dark one.

Not decided here

Whether the mascot uses the accent violet or a warmer colour of its own is still open. Mascots often sit outside the interface palette on purpose. What is settled is that the interface accent is violet, and that it is not the Dpro orange — an orange accent next to amber warnings would fight for the same meaning.
